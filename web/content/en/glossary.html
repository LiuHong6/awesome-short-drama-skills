Terms you will meet when making AI short dramas, in production order. Each term has its own link, for example /en/glossary/#manju.

Formats

Short drama (micro drama)
A serialized drama with episodes from tens of seconds to a few minutes, mostly in vertical format, usually tens to over a hundred episodes long. The first episodes are free; later ones are unlocked by paying per episode or watching ads. In China it airs mainly on Hongguo, Douyin and Kuaishou; overseas apps include ReelShort and DramaBox. Also called vertical drama or 短剧 / 微短剧.
Vertical drama
A drama shot or generated in the phone's portrait ratio (9:16). When generating with AI, state the aspect ratio in the prompt; many video models default to landscape.
Manju (AI manju, motion comic)
A serialized drama made of AI-generated anime or illustration-style images with voiceover, subtitles and simple motion. It needs no actors or shooting and costs less than live action; the hardest part is keeping each character looking the same across dozens of episodes. From the Chinese 漫剧.
Overseas short drama
A short drama made or localized for audiences outside China. Scripts need to fit local viewing habits, so they are usually rewritten to English screenwriting conventions rather than translated line by line.

Tools

Agent skill (SKILL.md)
A folder installed into an agent such as Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw, QwenWork or Doubao desktop. Chinese users also call it a 技能 or 技能包. At its core is a SKILL.md that spells out the steps, format and standards for a task; it can also ship templates and scripts. See what is an AI short drama skill.
Agent
An AI assistant that can read files, run commands and complete a task step by step, such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and OpenClaw, or Chinese agents such as QwenWork, Doubao desktop, WorkBuddy, Kimi Code and Trae. A skill is its set of working instructions.
OpenClaw (龙虾, "lobster")
An open-source AI agent, nicknamed "lobster" (龙虾) in China. Tencent's QClaw, Kimi's KimiClaw and other "claw" products are built on it. Its skills use the standard SKILL.md format, so the skills listed here install directly; see the install guide.
Workflow (pipeline)
Short drama production split into fixed steps, each handing its output to the next. A full-pipeline skill packages the whole line; a focused skill handles one or two steps.

Script

Adaptation
Turning a novel or web novel into a short drama script: cutting subplots, compressing the timeline, turning inner monologue into action and dialogue that can be filmed, and redistributing the plot across episodes.
Episode outline
Before writing scripts, a list of what happens in each episode and where it ends. Once the outline is set, the scripts are written episode by episode. From the Chinese 集纲.
Hook
Anything that makes viewers stop and keep watching. The opening hook grabs attention in the first seconds; the end hook is a cliffhanger that makes viewers tap the next episode.
Paywall point
Where the free episodes end and paid ones begin. It is usually placed at the peak of a conflict, when viewers most want to know what happens, and is a key part of short drama script design. From the Chinese 付费卡点.
Payoff beat (shuangdian)
A plot moment that gives viewers a rush of satisfaction: a comeback, a humiliation reversed, a hidden identity revealed, a twist. Fast-paced short dramas pack these beats densely. From the Chinese 爽点.
De-AI
Editing AI-written text to remove stock phrases, tricolons, empty adjectives and repetitive sentence patterns, so scripts and dialogue read as human-written. From the Chinese 去 AI 味.

Visuals

Storyboard
The script broken into individual shots, each with shot size, camera position and movement, what is on screen, dialogue and duration. Storyboarding before generating video saves a lot of generation cost.
Shot size and camera movement
Shot size is how much the frame shows (wide, full, medium, close-up, extreme close-up); camera movement is how the camera moves (push in, pull out, pan, track, follow). Both belong in storyboards and video prompts.
Character consistency
Keeping a character's face and costume the same across shots and episodes in AI generation. Common methods are fixed reference images, turnaround sheets and unchanging descriptions.
Turnaround sheet (character sheet)
Full-body front, side and back views of one character, often with expressions and details. Used as a reference image for later shots to keep the character consistent.
Video prompt
Text instructions for a video model describing the image, character action, camera movement, lighting and duration. Each model has its own syntax, which is why this site groups skills by video model.
Text-, image- and reference-to-video (T2V / I2V / R2V)
T2V generates video from text alone; I2V uses an image as the first frame; R2V gives the model several character or scene reference images and generates a video containing them. Manju relies on I2V and R2V to keep characters consistent.

Platforms and models

Hongguo
ByteDance's free short drama app, supported by ads. Many Chinese short drama skills are built around the pacing of hits on Hongguo and Douyin.
Seedance / Jimeng
Seedance is ByteDance's video generation model; Jimeng (Dreamina) is ByteDance's AI creation platform, where Seedance can be used. It is the model Chinese short drama skills target most often.
Kling
Kuaishou's video generation model and creation platform.
